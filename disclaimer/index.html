In plain words

Neurosurgery Plus is an educational tool. It is NOT a tool for making decisions about real patients.

  • It is not a medical device. No regulator (for example in the European Union, the United Kingdom or the United States) has assessed, certified or cleared it.
  • It can contain errors, and it can be incomplete or out of date. Always check the original publications.

  • The developer accepts no responsibility for any decision made, or any harm that follows, from using the app, to the fullest extent permitted by law.

If you do not accept this, please do not use the app.

1. Who the app is for

Neurosurgery Plus (the "App") is made for healthcare professionals and students in healthcare (for example neurosurgeons, residents, physicians, nurses, allied health professionals and medical students) who are at least 18 years old, as a study and reference aid.

The App is not intended for patients or the general public. If you are a patient or a relative, please do not use the App to understand, check or question your own care: talk to your doctor.

2. Educational purpose only

The only intended purpose of the App is education: helping users study, teach and revise neurosurgical knowledge, discover recent literature and find educational events.

The App is not intended, and must not be used, to:

  • diagnose, prevent, monitor, predict, prognosticate, treat or relieve any disease, injury or condition in a real person;
  • decide whether, when or how to operate, admit, discharge, image, refer or treat a patient;
  • calculate drug doses or any treatment parameter for a patient;
  • replace or check the judgement of a qualified clinician;
  • support decisions at the bedside, in the emergency room, in the operating room, in the ward, in clinic, or in any other clinical setting.

3. Scores: what the results mean and what they do not mean

The Scores section reproduces published grading systems, scales, classifications and scores as described in the medical literature, so that you can learn how they are built and how they are interpreted.

  • A result in the App is only a description of what the original scale or study reported, in the population and setting where it was developed. It does not apply automatically to any individual patient.
  • Scores simplify reality. They do not consider the whole clinical picture: your patient's history, examination, imaging, evolution, comorbidities, preferences and local resources.
  • A result suggesting a "low" grade or risk does not mean that a patient is safe, and a "high" grade or risk does not mean that an intervention is indicated or futile.
  • Different versions, modifications and interpretations of the same score exist in the literature. The App may show one version only, and the wording may be simplified for teaching.
  • Percentages, survival figures and risks come from published cohorts and may not reflect current practice or your setting.
  • Each score lists its references: read them. The original publication always prevails over the App.

If a score result and your clinical assessment disagree, trust your clinical assessment, and never withhold a test or treatment a patient needs because of a score.
